Operating model

From the current picture to a practical next action.

WAIA connects these parts of the work. Learning participation shows who has engaged; examples from real work show what may be changing. Management reviews both before deciding what to do.

01

Current picture

See what needs attention. Use AI Baseline to explore reported concerns where useful.

02

Guidance and People

Make local expectations visible and give people appropriate access to learning and support.

03

Real work

People share examples of AI use in actual tasks and workflows.

04

Evidence

Look at what changed, including checking, rework and uncertain outcomes.

05

Review

Consider strength, consistency and recency. Accountable managers validate conclusions.

06

Decision

Choose the next practical action, including where more evidence is needed.

Starts, completions and certificates describe learning participation. They do not prove workplace application, behaviour change or organisational improvement. WAIA keeps those signals separate from evidence about the work.

Evidence mechanics

WAIA separates evidence strength from value direction.

That separation is what keeps the product credible. A workflow can have strong evidence that AI is helping, strong evidence that AI is adding burden or insufficient evidence to decide yet.

Reported → Observed → Repeated → Validated

A single structured observation can become observed. Repeated evidence needs observations across time. Validated requires accountable human management review.

Positive, neutral, negative, mixed or unclear

WAIA doesn’t assume AI is creating value. Negative and mixed signals help management see where AI may be increasing checking, rework or risk.

Capacity discipline

Indicative capacity is evidence dependent.

Where evidence is strong enough, consistent and current, WAIA can show conservative indicative capacity ranges. Weak evidence, stale evidence, unclear outcomes or unsuitable workflow frequency can prevent calculation.

Illustrative example

A realistic evidence journey.

This is an illustrative scenario, not a customer case study. It shows the kind of operating question WAIA is designed to make visible.

01

AI is already in use

An organisation has introduced Copilot, while some teams also use ChatGPT for drafting, meeting notes and first-pass research. Usage spreads unevenly.

02

Evidence enters the flow

People record recurring workflows, frequency, net time change, checking and rework burden, outcome signal and plain workplace context.

03

Strength and direction separate

WAIA distinguishes Reported, Observed, Repeated and Validated evidence from whether the result is positive, neutral, negative, mixed or unclear.

04

Managers review what holds up

Stronger evidence can be grouped into organisation workflows, reviewed by accountable managers and used to decide what needs investment, intervention or more evidence.

05

Capacity stays conservative

Where the evidence supports it, WAIA can show indicative capacity. Where it is weak, mixed or stale, the product keeps that uncertainty visible.

Organisation journey

A practical start, not a transformation programme.

WAIA begins with the organisation's current questions, then makes Guidance, learning and evidence from real work useful for the people who need them.

01

Fit conversation

Discuss current AI use, the operating concern and who needs to be involved.

02

Confirm scope

Agree the eligible population, administrators, sponsor and first evidence priority.

03

Configure the organisation

Set the organisation view, Guidance, AI Baseline and initial learning pathway.

04

Use AI Baseline where useful

Review reported concerns and stronger areas as a starting point for follow-up, not proof of performance.

05

Publish guidance

Make practical expectations visible to the people who need to apply them.

06

Invite participants

Give the agreed people access to WAIA and appropriate learning.

07

Review signals

Review learning participation and Guidance acknowledgement as context. Use examples from real work and evidence strength to decide the next action.

Product boundaries

WAIA supports evidence-led AI management. It doesn’t take ownership away from the organisation.

WAIA doesn’t replace legal advice, regulatory advice, HR advice or compliance certification.

Governance ownership

WAIA doesn’t replace internal governance ownership, risk decisions, management conclusions or human judgement.

Technical monitoring

WAIA isn’t enterprise GRC, technical AI model monitoring or employee surveillance. It’s also not an autonomous ROI engine.
