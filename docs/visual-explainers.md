# WAIA visual explainer guidance

This is the **WAIA-specific configuration**, not a duplicate of the shared method. Use [the shared visual-explainer skill](https://github.com/bjcooper23-19-2/codex-operating-system/blob/main/skills/visual-explainer/SKILL.md) for process, creative exploration and quality gates.

## Authority and audience
Read [WAIA beliefs](./waia-beliefs.md) first, then [LinkedIn guidance](./linkedin.md) for social assets, and current website brand assets/style for visual identity. WAIA teaches UK SMB owners, managers and employees who may have heard AI terminology without understanding it. Assume low technical familiarity; never patronise the reader.

## Educational standard
- Start with one thing the reader should understand or be able to explain.
- Where the subject has unfamiliar vocabulary, pair **familiar analogy + real technical term + one-sentence meaning + everyday workplace example**. The analogy must clarify rather than distort the concept.
- Keep correct terms visible. Do not replace `triggers`, `LLM`, `guardrails` or `autonomy` with friendly metaphors alone.
- Explain in the language of work: a customer email, a calendar booking, a policy check, an invoice, a CRM record, or a management decision.
- Explain what the system can do and where human judgement, checking and accountability remain necessary.
- Avoid claiming guardrails guarantee safety, that AI literally thinks like a person, or that all agents have identical architecture.
- No arbitrary nine-card rule. Choose a diagram, annotated example, comparison, single graphic or carousel based on what teaches best.
- The graphic should stand alone without its LinkedIn caption; avoid overloading a phone screen.

## Visual direction
Use the **current** WAIA marketing identity and approved logo/colours/typography from the repository; inspect the assets before designing, rather than treating colours from a previous mock-up as canonical. Premium means deliberate hierarchy, restrained colour, clear typography, good spacing and polished illustration. Readability beats decoration. Allow variation in illustration and layout while retaining recognisable brand cues. Produce editable source where feasible, and check final export at mobile size.

## Editorial and governance
- Follow WAIA's People / Process / Data lens and 'visibility before control' position when relevant, without forcing a product pitch.
- No news-bulletin openings, jargon dumps, hype or unsupported statistics.
- Use credible sources for technical assertions; record caveats in the production handoff.
- Treat this as an occasional educational format, not a fixed posting cadence.
- Human approval is required before any scheduling or publishing.

## First acceptance example: Anatomy of an AI agent
**Learning outcome:** A non-technical business owner can explain how an AI agent starts a task, accesses information, works out next steps, takes action and knows when a person must intervene.

Potential mappings, subject to technical review:
- **Ears / Triggers:** a new customer email starts the work.
- **Eyes / Knowledge:** relevant customer records and documents provide information.
- **Memory / Memory:** saved context helps carry useful details between steps, when configured.
- **Heart / Goals and instructions:** define the task and what good looks like.
- **Brain / AI model (LLM):** interprets information and proposes next steps; it can be wrong.
- **Immune system / Guardrails:** restrictions and checks reduce risk, not guarantee safety.
- **Nerves / Connectors:** connections provide access to email or CRM systems.
- **Hands / Tools:** permitted operations such as drafting a reply or updating a ticket.
- **Legs / Autonomy:** how much the system may do before asking for approval; this is a design setting, not necessarily a discrete component.

These nine analogies are **reference content, not a compulsory visual template**. Challenge weak mappings (particularly 'heart', 'immune system' and 'legs') during design. A single customer-service scenario should connect the pieces so readers see a complete workflow rather than nine unrelated definitions.

**Acceptance:** Reader understands the core concept without a caption, recognises the technical terms, can give a workplace example, and is not misled about safety or independent decision-making.
