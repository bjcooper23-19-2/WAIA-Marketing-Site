# WAIA Website Messaging Guidance

This document defines how WAIA's wider doctrine should be expressed through the public website.

It is subordinate to [`docs/waia-beliefs.md`](./waia-beliefs.md). If website copy conflicts with the belief system, the belief system wins.

## Role Of The Website

The website should explain the problem WAIA helps solve in clear commercial language.

It should not reduce WAIA to another AI training, prompt, governance or productivity product.

The broader position is:

**WAIA helps SMBs navigate a fast-moving transformation in how work gets done through People, Process and Data.**

A practical expression is:

**WAIA helps SMBs turn informal experimentation with new technology into repeatable, evidence-led ways of improving work.**

The site should make that broader position understandable without becoming abstract.

## Messaging Priorities

Website copy should consistently answer four questions:

### 1. What is changing?

Work is changing quickly as new technology becomes part of everyday activity.

The challenge is not simply access to tools. It is how roles, workflows, judgement, management expectations and operating practices change around them.

### 2. Why is that difficult for SMBs?

Smaller organisations often experience this change informally and unevenly.

Common problems include:

- different teams using different approaches
- uncertainty about what is actually helping
- weak visibility into checking and rework
- unclear expectations for managers and employees
- experimentation that never becomes repeatable practice
- governance that either arrives too late or becomes unnecessarily heavy
- claimed time savings with no plan for redeployment

### 3. What does WAIA help them do?

WAIA should be framed as helping organisations:

- understand what is already changing
- build better judgement and confidence
- improve important workflows
- capture credible evidence from real work
- identify what is worth standardising
- understand where capacity is being released
- make better management decisions about what to do next

### 4. What is the outcome?

The outcome is not "more AI".

The outcome is better work: more consistent, more deliberate, more evidence-led and better aligned with business priorities.

## People. Process. Data.

Where useful, the website should make the WAIA operating lens visible.

### People

How roles, skills, confidence, judgement, trust and management behaviour are changing.

### Process

How workflows, hand-offs, review points, standards and operating practices are changing.

### Data

What evidence shows whether the change is worthwhile and what management should do next.

This should feel practical rather than like a consulting framework added for decoration.

## Homepage Guidance

The homepage should avoid leading with a generic promise such as:

- adopt AI faster
- unlock AI productivity
- train your teams to use AI
- maximise AI ROI

Those messages are too narrow and too easy to confuse with other providers.

The stronger lead is the transformation of work, with AI introduced as an important part of the context.

The homepage should communicate:

- the pace and complexity of change
- the informal nature of adoption in many SMBs
- the need to understand People, Process and Data together
- the need to move from experimentation to repeatable practice
- the importance of evidence and management decisions

## Product Messaging

Product copy should explain what a feature helps the organisation understand, decide or improve.

Prefer outcome-led explanations such as:

- establish a baseline
- capture evidence from real work
- identify recurring friction
- improve judgement
- surface checking and rework
- understand capacity signals
- support management decisions

Avoid feature copy that implies activity itself is success, such as:

- drive usage
- maximise adoption
- increase prompt activity
- monitor employees

unless the surrounding context makes the business purpose explicit.

## Proof And Claims

WAIA should remain evidence-led in its own marketing.

Do not claim:

- guaranteed productivity gains
- guaranteed ROI
- direct headcount reduction
- precise value from hours saved without evidence of redeployment
- transformation outcomes that have not been demonstrated

Where evidence is partial, say so.

Use proof to show credible progress, not certainty that the product cannot support.

## Language Principle

Use **AI** when it is genuinely the subject, when clarity requires it or when search intent makes it useful.

Do not force the term into every heading, paragraph or CTA.

Prefer the language of:

- work
- transformation
- workflows
- judgement
- capacity
- evidence
- management
- culture
- automation
- operating practices
- organisational learning

Do not replace AI with vague wording when the reader needs to know what technology is being discussed.

## CTA Guidance

Calls to action should match the problem WAIA solves.

Prefer invitations to:

- understand where the organisation is now
- establish a baseline
- see what should change next
- explore how WAIA works
- review current operating practices

Avoid generic pressure such as:

- transform now
- unlock AI today
- don't get left behind

The tone should support deliberate action rather than urgency for its own sake.

## Website Audit Test

When reviewing a page, ask:

1. Does it describe a real change in how work gets done?
2. Does it make the People, Process and Data perspective visible where useful?
3. Does it make AI the catalyst rather than the hero?
4. Does it explain what the organisation should understand, improve or decide?
5. Does it avoid unsupported productivity and ROI claims?
6. Does it sound distinct from generic AI training and governance providers?
7. Is the language commercially clear rather than abstract?

If a page fails several of these tests, it should be reviewed against the doctrine before further optimisation.
