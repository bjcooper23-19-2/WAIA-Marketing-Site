# WAIA Outbound Messaging Guidance

This document defines how WAIA's wider doctrine should be expressed through cold email, Apollo sequences, local outreach and other first-contact sales messaging.

It is subordinate to [`docs/waia-beliefs.md`](./waia-beliefs.md). If an outbound tactic conflicts with the belief system, the belief system wins.

## Role Of Outbound

Outbound should create relevance quickly without collapsing WAIA into generic AI training, adoption or productivity language.

The objective is not to explain the whole doctrine in one email. It is to identify a credible business problem, show that WAIA understands the operational reality behind it, and make the next conversation worthwhile.

## Core Commercial Frame

A useful starting point is:

**Many SMBs are already using new technology informally. The harder part is understanding what is actually improving, what is creating extra work, and which practices are worth standardising.**

WAIA helps organisations navigate that change through **People, Process and Data.**

The most commercially relevant problems usually sit in one or more of these areas:

### People

- employees using tools with uneven confidence
- managers unsure what good use looks like
- changing expectations without clear guidance
- hidden or informal use
- anxiety, trust or capability gaps

### Process

- fragmented approaches to the same work
- isolated task gains with more checking elsewhere
- experimentation that never becomes repeatable practice
- unclear review points
- time saved without a plan for redeployment

### Data

- no credible view of what is working
- reliance on anecdotes or usage numbers
- unclear evidence of quality, rework or capacity
- weak visibility into what should be scaled, stopped or standardised

## Message Construction

A strong cold message should normally contain four parts:

1. **Relevant observation**
   A credible reason this problem may matter to the recipient or their organisation.

2. **Operational tension**
   The harder issue beneath simple adoption, such as inconsistent ways of working, management uncertainty, checking and rework, or weak evidence of value.

3. **WAIA's role**
   A concise explanation of how WAIA helps organisations understand and improve the situation.

4. **Low-friction next step**
   An invitation to explore relevance, not a forced demo pitch.

## Preferred Problem Language

Prefer language such as:

- how work is changing
- informal experimentation
- inconsistent ways of working
- what is actually improving
- where extra checking or rework appears
- which practices are worth standardising
- where capacity is genuinely being released
- what managers should do next
- evidence from real work
- confidence and judgement

Use **AI** when it is useful for clarity and relevance, but do not repeat it in every line.

## Claims And Boundaries

Do not claim or imply that WAIA will automatically:

- deliver a fixed productivity percentage
- produce guaranteed ROI
- reduce headcount
- eliminate risk
- prove value from usage alone

Do not present hours saved as realised financial value unless there is evidence of how that capacity was redeployed.

Outbound should sound commercially credible, not inflated.

## CTA Guidance

Prefer low-friction language such as:

- worth comparing notes?
- useful to see how you're approaching this?
- happy to show you the framework we're using
- worth a short conversation if this is becoming a live issue

Avoid:

- book a demo now
- transform your business today
- unlock AI ROI
- don't get left behind
- generic "interested in learning more?" endings with no clear relevance

The CTA should feel like a logical continuation of the problem described.

## Personalisation

Personalisation should strengthen relevance, not become decoration.

Useful signals include:

- company size and growth stage
- hiring or operating changes
- evidence of AI or automation initiatives
- leadership commentary on productivity, skills or transformation
- industry-specific workflow pressure
- local relevance where appropriate

Do not pretend to know internal problems that are not evidenced.

Phrase uncertainty honestly.

For example, prefer:

> I don't know whether this is showing up in your teams yet, but we're seeing a common pattern...

rather than asserting an unverified pain point as fact.

## Sequence Discipline

Across a sequence, vary the angle rather than repeating the same claim.

A useful progression is:

- message 1: the operational problem
- message 2: a People, Process or Data angle not covered in the first
- message 3: evidence, practical observation or misconception
- message 4: concise close-out with a clear reason to respond or decline

Do not make every follow-up another version of "just checking this reached you".

## Decision Test

Before sending or approving a sequence, check:

1. Does the message lead with a real business issue rather than AI for its own sake?
2. Does it reflect People, Process or Data?
3. Is the problem credible for the target without pretending to know too much?
4. Is the value proposition broader than training, prompts or adoption?
5. Are claims proportionate and evidence-led?
6. Is the CTA natural and commercially useful?
7. Does the message sound like a senior operator rather than generic sales automation?

If the answer is mostly no, the message should be rewritten before use.
